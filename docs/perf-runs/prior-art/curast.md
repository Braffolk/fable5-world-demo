# Prior-art brief: CuRast (Schütz et al. / Fellner & Behnke)

- **Source**: `https://github.com/m-schuetz/CuRast` — "CuRast: Cuda-Based Software Rasterization for Billions of Triangles" (arXiv 2604.21749).
- **Fetched**: `git clone --depth 1` succeeded into `docs/perf-runs/prior-art/sources/curast` (repo ships the arXiv PDF + GLB demo asset; ~135 MB). Paper text extracted with `pdftotext -layout` → `/tmp/curast_paper.txt` (the Read PDF path needs `pdftoppm`, which is absent; `pdftotext` worked). Hot code read directly.
- **What it is**: A pure-CUDA Nanite-style **visibility-buffer software rasterizer** for hundreds-of-millions-to-billions of micro-triangles from photogrammetry/reconstruction. Same family as our system; the README explicitly builds on Nanite + FreePipe + CUDARaster. NOTE: it targets **dense OPAQUE** geometry (no holes/overdraw the way our holey foliage has); transparency is a separate experimental pipeline. So the opaque-path techniques transfer as *principles*, and the **translucent** pipeline is the most architecturally on-point for our holey-overdraw problem.

## Raster architecture (3 stages, by triangle size)

Single visibility buffer, `uint64_t* framebuffer`, depth in MSBs, id in LSBs, **64-bit `atomicMin` per covered fragment** (`pack_pixel`, `rasterization_helpers.cuh:35`). Work is split by **screen-space bbox fragment count**:

- **Stage 1 — small (<128 frag bbox), 1 thread/triangle** (`triangles_visbuffer.cu:84-312`, `316-426`). Persistent cooperative-grid kernel; grid sized to `cuOccupancyMaxActiveBlocksPerMultiprocessor * numSMs` (`CudaModularProgram.h:560-584`). Each 256-thread block claims a batch of 256 triangles via `atomicAdd(numProcessedBatches, 1)` (`:352`), walks meshes by cumulative count. Per thread: transform 3 verts → NDC, view-space backface cull, bbox, **incremental barycentric add-step raster over the bbox** (no edge walk), `atomicMin`. Triangles too big or near-plane-crossing are pushed to a global queue for stage 2.
- **Stage 2 — medium (<4096 frag), 1 warp/triangle** (`:430-616`). Lane 0 does setup (incl. Sutherland-Hodgman near-plane clip for an accurate visible bbox), shuffles to the warp; 32 lanes stride the bbox. Bigger-than-tile or near-crossing → split into 64×64 tiles, queued to stage 3.
- **Stage 3 — huge, 1 block(64thr)/tile** (`:621-804`). Per-pixel **ray-triangle intersection in world space** (Möller-Trumbore) instead of barycentric raster, specifically to avoid near-plane clipping math.
- **Resolve** (`resolve.cu`): 1 thread/pixel, decode id, **binary-search the mesh** via prefix-sum of triangle counts, re-fetch + re-project + shade **in world space** (so mip/derivatives work for near-plane tris).

**Measured cost shape (paper Table 3, RTX 4090, Zorah 13.6B vis-tris):** Stage1 = 74–99 ms, Stage2 = 0.13–0.19 ms, Stage3 = 0.18–0.21 ms, Resolve = 0.25–0.42 ms. **Stage 1 (the 1-thread small-triangle raster) is ~99% of frame time.** This is exactly our regime: the per-thread micro-triangle path *is* the cost, and stages 2/3 + resolve are noise. Validates that our optimization energy belongs in the small-triangle transform+coverage path, not in big-triangle handling or the resolve.

## The 64-bit-atomic question (central to us)

CuRast's depth election is a **single 64-bit `atomicMin(uint64_t)`** — depth(28b, MSB)|id(36b) — on **every** in-triangle fragment (`triangles_visbuffer.cu:290-293`). All four raster paths do this (`grep atomicMin`). It is the *opposite* of portable to WGSL: it is the very 64-bit interleaved depth+payload trick we cannot use, and the paper's §3.1 narrates precisely our problem — FreePipe (2010) was stuck on **32-bit atomics**, "which made it difficult to attach information in addition to the mandatory depth value," and they note FreePipe's workaround was **two 32-bit atomic-mins** (one depth, one payload — racy unless serialized). Our packed 24b-depth|8b-tiebreak `atomicMax` + winner-store side buffer is a *strictly better* 32-bit adaptation than what the literature offered, so there is **nothing to port** from CuRast's election; confirm it's a dead branch for us. **Notable contrast / possible lever:** CuRast fires the atomic *unconditionally* on every covered fragment (no pre-load). We already do better (relaxed `atomicLoad` + early-out, atomic only on a new front). So our coverage loop's atomic discipline is *ahead* of CuRast — do not regress it.

## Most transferable techniques

### DIRECT ports (adapt to WGSL, quality-preserving)

1. **Sample-miss tiny-triangle cull (best free win).** `triangles_visbuffer.cu:190-196`: before rasterizing, if the bbox is sub-pixel and lies strictly *between* sample centers on x or y (`min_x > floor(min_x)+OFFSET && max_x < floor(min_x)+1+OFFSET`), the triangle covers no sample → skip *before* the coverage loop. Paper Table 4: **74→102 ms and 99→140 ms** on Zorah = **27–40% of the whole frame**, zero quality change (it would have rasterized nothing). Our ~1px holey-foliage tris are exactly the population that falls between samples. This is a handful of comparisons in per-triangle setup, attacking BOTH our 60% coverage (kills the loop) AND our 40% setup (cheap early-out). **Highest-priority transfer.** Citation: `:190-196` + Table 4.

2. **`SAMPLE_OFFSET` placement: bake the −0.5 into screen coords, do NOT add 0.5 in the loop.** `triangles_visbuffer.cu:166-172`: they explicitly measured that a per-pixel `+0.5` sample offset is **up to 40% slower** (Zorah 74→102 ms) than subtracting 0.5 once from the screen-space vertices. Pure codegen/register effect. Check whether our coverage loop adds a per-fragment 0.5 we could hoist into vertex setup — potentially a large, free win on the dominant loop.

3. **`__fdividef`-style fast reciprocal to cut register pressure → occupancy.** `:244,247-249,287` + paper §4.1: switching the depth/bary divisions to the fast intrinsic dropped register use **55→48** and improved occupancy (not just raw divide speed). WGSL adaptation: we can't pick the intrinsic, but the *principle* — minimize live registers in the coverage loop to raise occupancy — is directly actionable. Audit `nanRasterWorld1` register/occupancy; precompute reciprocals (`inv_z_a/b/c`) once outside the loop (we already do z, check for others) and prefer multiply-by-reciprocal over divide. Indirect effect on the 60% loop via more resident warps hiding the relaxed-load latency.

4. **Incremental add-step barycentric raster, NOT edge-walk.** `:251-310`: precompute `ds_dx, ds_dy, dt_dx, dt_dy` outside the loop; inside the loop barycentrics advance by **addition only** (`s += ds_dx`), inside-test is 3 sign checks on `s,t,v=1-s-t`. This is the cheapest possible per-fragment coverage test and is what we should be doing (verify our loop is add-stepped, not recomputing edge functions per pixel). Note `v = 1-(s+t)` reuses the other two, so only 2 of the 3 "edges" cost an add.

### INDIRECT (adapt-the-principle)

5. **Per-tile binning + sort + bounded front-to-back blend with opacity early-out (the translucent pipeline).** `triangles_translucent.cu` + `CuRast_render.h:162-264`: stage1 bins each triangle into 16×16 tiles (`atomicAdd(queueSize, numTiles)`), key = (tile_x,tile_y,depth); **CUB radix-sort the (tile|depth) keys globally**; `kernel_stage3_computeRanges` finds per-tile [start,end) ranges from the sorted list; `kernel_stage4_blend` runs **one block per tile**, prefetches 256 triangles to shared mem, and blends **front-to-back** maintaining `remainingTranslucency`, accumulating until coverage saturates (`:558-668`). **Why this matters for holey foliage:** our 12–20× overdraw on *opaque* leaf cards is functionally an order-independent depth race that revisits the same pixel many times. If we (a) bin foliage tris into screen tiles and (b) process a tile front-to-back, an **opaque** pixel can **terminate the moment it is first covered** — every later (farther) fragment for that pixel is skipped *before* its coverage/depth work, directly cutting the dominant 60% loop by ~the overdraw factor. Front-to-back + first-opaque-wins is **exactly equivalent** to a depth buffer for opaque geometry → zero quality loss (NOT the forbidden quantile/occlusion popping; nothing behind a real cover is shown, holes still show through because a hole emits no fragment to terminate on). Cost: a global sort + tile ranges. This is the one *big-refactor* candidate worth modeling. Adaptation hurdles for us: needs a binning pass + a GPU sort (CUB→WGSL radix sort or a coarse depth-bucket count-sort, since we only need approximate front-to-back per tile, not exact); the per-tile shared-mem prefetch loop maps cleanly to a workgroup-per-tile WGSL dispatch. The "stash 4 nearest fragments, blend min" trick (`:585-601`) is a translucent detail we don't need (opaque = take first).

6. **Persistent occupancy-bound grid + global atomic work-queue, vs one-dispatch-per-cluster.** `CudaModularProgram.h:560-584` + `triangles_visbuffer.cu:346-377`: launch only `~occupancy*numSMs` blocks; each block loops, pulling 256-triangle batches via `atomicAdd` until the global triangle list is exhausted. This is *load-balancing across uneven mesh/cluster sizes with no host-side dispatch-per-cluster*. Our "one workgroup per visible cluster" (~154k workgroups) pays launch/scheduling overhead and gives the scheduler no slack to balance the long tail. WGSL adaptation: WebGPU has no cooperative-groups `grid.sync()` and no guaranteed forward-progress across workgroups, but the **persistent-thread pattern works within a single dispatch** using a storage-buffer atomic counter as the work cursor: dispatch a fixed modest number of workgroups (≈ SM count × resident-wg estimate), each loops `atomicAdd(&cursor, BATCH)` over the global cluster/triangle list until drained. This collapses 154k workgroup launches into ~hundreds of persistent workgroups and balances the well-filled-but-variable clusters — attacks the 40% transform+launch. Caveat: must ensure no cross-workgroup barrier is needed (CuRast's `grid.sync()` is only for the one-time gridwide counter init, which we'd do in a tiny separate pass or via the host). Risk: WebGPU spec gives no forward-progress guarantee between workgroups; a starved persistent workgroup can't deadlock here because there's no inter-wg wait, only independent draining — safe.

7. **Vertex-reuse / load-once-per-warp to cut redundant fetch (our ~5× re-fetch + ~48 wind taps).** Paper §6 / Future Work cites Karis's meshlet approach: "load vertex/index data cooperatively, store in shared memory, then process one-thread-per-triangle." CuRast's **instancing** path already embodies the principle (`:415-422`, §4.5): load+transform a triangle's verts **once**, then loop over instances reusing them — and they note this is a *bandwidth* win, the stated "major bottleneck during rasterization." Our cost map flags ~5× redundant `fetchWorldVert` + ~48 texture taps for wind animation per triangle: the transferable principle is **cooperatively load the cluster's unique vertices (and their wind-animated world positions) into workgroup shared memory ONCE, then have threads index shared memory per-triangle**, eliminating the 5× re-fetch and de-duplicating wind taps to one-per-unique-vertex. This is the cleanest attack on the 40% transform+launch and is independent of the raster loop. (Direct in spirit; "indirect" only because CuRast applies it via instancing, not shared-mem vertex cache — but the meshlet shared-mem cache is named as their own future work.)

## Things CuRast CONFIRMS as non-wins (do not resurface; flag the confirmation)

- **Edge-walk / inside-triangle-only traversal (Pineda) is NOT worth it for dense micro-tris.** Paper §4 (`:255-261`) and §6 (`:584-589`): they *tried* limiting traversal to fragments inside the triangle and found "the additional effort to avoid wasted work is often more expensive than unnecessary but cheap work, especially for massive, dense data sets that produce pixel-sized triangles." They couldn't get a win and leave it as open future work, hinting it "may be more advantageous for less dense geometry." **This directly bears on our shipped scanline x-span (commit 19eb834) and our reverted depth-DDA**: CuRast confirms the per-fragment-savings-vs-setup-overhead tradeoff is marginal-to-negative at ~1px tris. Treat further coverage-traversal micro-opt as low-EV; do not gold-plate it.
- **Conservative occlusion culling**: CuRast does *none* (opaque dense, brute-force atomicMin) and still hits its targets — consistent with our finding that HZB occlusion is ~0% on holey foliage. No new occlusion idea here.
- **Sub-pixel LOD over-render**: CuRast has no LOD at all (no precomputed LODs by design). Nothing to add; our cluster cut at ≤1px is already past what CuRast does.

## Net read for our cost map

- **60% per-pixel coverage loop:** biggest swing is **#5 (tile-bin + front-to-back + first-opaque early-out)** — the only technique here that structurally removes overdraw work rather than shaving per-fragment cost. Cheaper immediate wins: **#1 sample-miss cull** (also helps), **#2 sample-offset hoist**, **#4** verify add-step loop.
- **40% transform+launch:** **#6 persistent work-queue** (kill 154k launches, balance the tail) + **#7 cooperative shared-mem vertex/wind cache** (kill 5× re-fetch + dedupe wind taps).
- **Election:** nothing portable; our 32-bit packed-atomicMax + winner-store is already strictly better than the literature's 32-bit options, and CuRast's whole election is the 64-bit trick we can't use. Our relaxed-load-then-conditional-atomic is *ahead* of CuRast's unconditional atomicMin — keep it.
