## Why this source is the most relevant of the set
The headline result is measured on SAN MIGUEL — a vegetation scene of **very small triangles (2.4 px/tri avg, 25% visible after cull)** — exactly our regime (holey foliage leaf-cards, ~1px tris, heavy overdraw). Their entire architecture exists *because* hardware raster of sub-pixel tris wastes ~4× on mandatory 2×2 quads (the same reason our SW raster exists). It is the textbook bin → coarse-tile → fine-pixel sort-middle pipeline and it directly attacks BOTH our cost centers: the 60% per-fragment coverage loop and the 40% transform/launch.

## Raster architecture (4 kernels, sort-middle, paper §4–5)
1. **Triangle setup** (`TriangleSetup.inl`): 1 thread : 1 input triangle (1:1, order-preserving). Cull (frustum/backface/**between-samples**), snap to 4-bit subpixel fixed-point, build plane equations for z/w, 1/w, u/w, v/w, store per-tri **zmin** for hier-Z. Output `CRTriangleHeader` (S16 screen verts + zmin + per-edge "flipbits") and `CRTriangleData` (the z and barycentric plane equations).
2. **Bin rasterizer** (`BinRaster.inl`): persistent CTAs (one per "bin stream", 16 total), each owns its own set of per-bin queues so **no inter-CTA sync on queue writes**. Bins = 128×128 px (16×16 tiles). Consumes input in big batches via one global atomic per batch. Trivial-accept for tris whose AABB ≤ 2×2 bins (the common case) — skips edge tests entirely.
3. **Coarse rasterizer** (`CoarseRaster.inl`): persistent CTA per SM, one bin at a time, merges the 16 per-bin queues (per-segment, not per-tri, because each segment is a contiguous input run). Produces per-**tile** (8×8 px) queues. **Load-balances emits**: counts total tile-intersections for the whole CTA, then spreads those write-tasks evenly across all 512 threads, each thread binary-searching which tile/warp/triangle its task index belongs to (`CoarseRaster.inl:572-628`).
4. **Fine rasterizer** (`FineRaster.inl`): one **warp per 8×8 tile**, persistent. Tile color+depth held in **shared memory** (`s_tileColor/s_tileDepth`, `FineRaster.inl:510-511`). Two-phase loop: (a) *input phase* — 32 lanes each compute a **64-bit coverage mask** for one queued triangle via LUT, compact-away empties, accumulate a running fragment count; (b) *shading phase* — once ≥32 fragments are queued, redistribute **exactly 32 fragments to 32 lanes** (fragments from possibly-different triangles), then per-lane do depth test → shade → ROP. This is the crux: coverage and shading are *decoupled and density-flattened*.

## Depth/visibility ELECTION and the no-64-bit-atomic question (critical for us)
CudaRaster does **NOT** use a depth|payload InterlockedMax visbuffer at all — so the "no 64-bit atomic" problem we worry about simply does not arise here, and there is nothing to port for that mechanism. Instead:
- Depth is a **32-bit value per pixel held in shared memory** for the warp's 8×8 tile (`s_tileDepth`). Depth is computed from an integer plane equation: `depth = zx*pixelX + zy*pixelY + zb` (`FineRaster.inl:702`, layout `PrivateDefs.hpp:51-57`). The whole 32-bit range is used for depth precision (`Constants.hpp:92-94`).
- The depth test is a plain shared-memory read-compare (`FineRaster.inl:703-705`); the winner does a plain shared write. **Correct ordering within a 32-fragment batch is achieved without atomics** by exploiting deterministic warp last-writer-wins: lanes are ordered so a later triangle's lane has higher index, and on GF100 the highest lane wins a conflicting shared write (`runFragmentShader`/`executeROP_SingleSample`, `FineRaster.inl:439-501`; paper §5.4 "ROP"). When depth/blend ordering matters they reverse lanes and loop-until-write-succeeds reading back the written depth.
- **Why no atomic is needed**: a tile is owned *exclusively* by one warp (picked via a single global atomic on `fineCounter`, `FineRaster.inl:548`). There is no cross-warp contention on any pixel — the only conflict is *intra-warp* among ≤32 fragments, resolved by lane ordering. This is the structural alternative to our atomicMax election: **partition the screen so each pixel is touched by exactly one execution unit, making the depth election a private, non-atomic operation.**

This is the single most important architectural contrast: we currently spend the 60% per-fragment loop doing, for every one of ~36M visible-tri fragments, a relaxed `atomicLoad` of a global election word + compare. CudaRaster never touches a global word per fragment — depth lives in on-chip shared memory and is private to the tile's warp.

## Transferable techniques

### DIRECT ports (mechanism transfers to WGSL with modest effort)

**T1 — "Between-samples" small-triangle cull in setup (`prepareTriangle`, `TriangleSetup.inl:60-108`).**
After snapping verts to fixed-point, snap the AABB to the sample grid; if the snapped lo>hi in x or y, the triangle's AABB falls entirely between sample centers → cull (produces zero fragments). For AABBs covering only 1–2 samples, evaluate those samples' coverage directly and cull if uncovered. Paper §6 Table 3: this cuts SAN MIGUEL's downstream triangle count by **3.3×** ("output at most one triangle per sample"). For our foliage of ~1px leaf-card tris this is potentially the largest single win — it removes tris that currently survive into our per-fragment loop and contribute overdraw without ever winning a pixel. *Adapt*: do it in our cluster/triangle setup before any fragment work; pure integer fixed-point math, no atomics, fully WGSL-portable. **Quality-preserving** (it only culls tris that cover no sample center — exact, not a quantile).

**T2 — LUT-based 8×8 coverage mask instead of per-fragment edge tests (`cover8x8_*`, `Util.inl:159-282`; paper Fig.3, §5.4).**
For each of the 3 edges, reduce to "edge slope in [0,1] via swap/mirror flipbits" then fetch a precomputed coverage bitmask from a small shared-memory LUT (6 KB, two half-lookups). AND the three edge masks → full 64-bit coverage of the 8×8 stamp in ~51 instr/edge, computed **once per triangle**, not per pixel. This is the direct antidote to our "3 integer edge tests per covered fragment": we currently re-derive coverage per-fragment; they derive it per-triangle-per-tile and then just iterate set bits. *Adapt*: WGSL has no 64-bit ints for the mask, but an 8×8 stamp = two `u32`s (lo/hi), and `countOneBits`/manual bit-iteration replace `__popcll`/`findBit`. The LUT lives in a `var<workgroup>` array. This is a tile-pipeline technique — it presupposes T5 (per-tile warp), so treat T2+T5 as a package.

**T3 — Hierarchical-Z kill via per-triangle zmin vs per-tile zmax (`earlyZCull`, `FineRaster.inl:228-239`; `updateTileZMax` 175-190).**
Each triangle carries a conservative `zmin` (computed in setup, stored in header `misc` field). Each tile-warp maintains `zmax` = farthest depth currently in its 8×8 tile, refreshed by a warp-reduction only when a depth that *equals* the current zmax is overwritten. A triangle whose `zmin >= tileZmax` is skipped wholesale before coverage. Paper Table 3: 15–37% of tris killed this way. **NOTE — this is the *conservative* hier-Z we already refuted at the cluster/sphere-vs-HZB level for holey foliage** (a gap pins zmax to far plane). The difference here is *granularity*: it operates per-8×8-tile with the *exact* zmax of already-shaded fragments in that very tile, not a coarse HZB. On holey foliage a tile that is fully painted by near leaves WILL have a near zmax and will then reject far tris behind it — so this can still pay off at tile granularity even though it's worthless at cluster granularity. It is exactly conservative (no popping). Worth re-measuring specifically as a *per-tile* test, separate from the refuted coarse-HZB.

**T4 — Drop barycentric/attribute plane-equation setup when not needed; defer attribute interpolation off the per-fragment path.**
Paper §6 Table 2: disabling color writes (skipping bary plane-eq setup + interpolation) improves their SW pipeline by **20–30%**, because attribute fetch+interpolation is "one of our biggest weaknesses" (§6.1). In setup they only build u/w,v/w,1/w pleqs if `EnableLerp` (`TriangleSetup.inl:168-179`); the fragment shader fetches verts and interpolates from pleqs (`FineRaster.inl:91-125`), never re-fetching geometry per fragment. *Maps onto our 40% transform+launch directly*: we do `3× fetchWorldVert` (~5× redundant re-fetch, ~48 wind texture taps) **per triangle inside the hot kernel**. The principle: compute plane-equation coefficients for whatever the visbuffer needs ONCE per triangle in setup; in the coverage/election loop carry only the packed depth-key and a triangle id, and resolve all wind-animated/world attributes in a *separate deferred resolve pass* over visible pixels (which we partly do). The lever is to ensure NOTHING attribute-ish (especially the 48 wind taps) happens per-triangle-per-tile in the election loop.

### INDIRECT ports (adapt the principle; bigger refactors)

**T5 — Sort-middle tile pipeline with per-tile warp + shared-memory depth (the whole architecture; paper §3.2–4, §5.4).**
This is the big one and the direct structural answer to our dominant 60% loop. Instead of one-workgroup-per-cluster splatting fragments into a global atomic visbuffer, **bin triangles into screen tiles, then give each tile to one workgroup that holds its depth (and id) buffer in `var<workgroup>` shared memory** and resolves visibility privately. Consequences that attack our exact costs:
  - The per-fragment global `atomicLoad`+compare (our 60%) becomes a shared-memory read-compare — orders of magnitude cheaper, and the depth election needs no atomic at all (see "Election" above), sidestepping the no-64-bit-atomic constraint entirely.
  - Overdraw is resolved *within on-chip memory*: 12–20× overdraw means 12–20× shared reads, not 12–20× global-atomic round-trips.
  - Coverage is computed once-per-tri-per-tile (T2), not per-fragment.
  *Adapt to WGSL*: tile = 8×8 (256 px × (depth u32 + id u32) = 2 KB workgroup mem, very comfortable; could go 16×16 = 8 KB). Election word stays our packed `(24b depthkey | 8b tiebreak)` u32, but now in workgroup memory; the id side-store also workgroup-local; write the winning id out at tile-flush. Intra-workgroup conflicts on the same pixel are resolved by a workgroup-scoped `atomicMax` on the shared word (WGSL *does* support 32-bit `atomic<u32>` in workgroup address space) — far cheaper than global. This is a substantial rewrite of `nanRasterWorld1` but is the highest-ceiling lever and is the canonical design for our exact workload. Caveat the paper itself flags (§6.2): every non-culled tri must traverse bin+coarse even if it ends up 1px — there is a fixed per-tri cost; mitigate with T1 (kill sub-sample tris before binning) and large batches.

**T6 — Decouple coverage from shading and flatten fragment density to full warps (fine rasterizer two-phase, `FineRaster.inl:597-738`; paper Fig.2).**
They never run "one thread per triangle then loop its pixels" (that is FreePipe, and the paper shows it underutilizes badly with mixed tri sizes — §2, §6, Fig.5). Instead: phase A computes coverage masks for 32 tris in parallel and packs a running fragment count; phase B pulls *exactly 32 fragments* (from any mix of triangles) onto 32 lanes for depth+shade. Result: shading always runs full-warp regardless of per-tri pixel-count variance. *Adapt*: our tris are uniformly ~1px so density-flattening across triangles within a tile is the relevant form — gather covered pixels from many tiny tris in a tile into dense lane-packed batches before the depth/shade step, so we never waste lanes on 1px tris. In WGSL this is a workgroup-level prefix-sum (`scan32_value`, `FineRaster.inl:308-317`) + bit-find to map a flat fragment index back to (triangle, pixel). Directly attacks idle-lane waste — though our note says clusters are well-filled, the *fragment*-level packing here is a different axis than cluster fill.

**T7 — Persistent threads + big-batch global-atomic work intake, replacing one-workgroup-per-cluster (paper §5.2; `BinRaster.inl:79-92`, fine `FineRaster.inl:542-554`).**
Launch a fixed number of persistent workgroups (≈ #SMs worth) that loop pulling work via a single global `atomicAdd` counter, consuming **large batches** to amortize the atomic. This is the antidote to our launch overhead of ~154k one-workgroup-per-cluster dispatches contributing to the 40% transform+launch. *Adapt*: WGSL supports global `atomic<u32>` add; a persistent-workgroup design (fixed grid, internal `loop` grabbing batches of clusters/triangles) is portable and removes per-cluster launch cost + improves load balance for the long-tail clusters. Note our memory says submit/BFS-pass batching was "measured marginal" — but that was about *submit* batching, not about replacing the launch model with persistent threads pulling batched work; this is a different, untested lever.

**T8 — Load-balanced emit distribution via flatten-then-binary-search (`CoarseRaster.inl:572-628`; paper §5.3).**
When per-element output count varies wildly (some tris hit many tiles), don't write "one element per thread"; instead count the CTA's *total* emits, assign emit-task indices 0..N-1 round-robin to all threads, and each thread binary-searches the prefix-sum tree to discover which (tile, warp, triangle) its task belongs to. Keeps every lane busy under skew. *Adapt (principle)*: wherever our pipeline has a variable fan-out step (e.g. a cluster/tri touching a variable number of tiles, or variable fragments), prefer flatten-total + per-task binary-search over per-producer loops. Pure index math, fully WGSL-portable; relevant if we adopt T5's binning.

## DEAD-END confirmations (source agrees these are not the lever for us)
- **Conservative occlusion/HZB at coarse granularity**: the paper's hier-Z is deliberately *per-tile and exact* (T3), not a coarse conservative HZB; consistent with our finding that coarse sphere-vs-maxZ HZB is ~0% on holey foliage. (Confirms; the per-tile variant is a *different* test, not the refuted one.)
- **Sub-pixel LOD over-render**: their setup *culls* sub-sample tris (T1) rather than rendering them — same spirit as our ≤1px cluster cut; confirms over-rendering below 1px is waste.
- **SW atomic-contention as the bottleneck**: CudaRaster avoids per-fragment atomics entirely by tiling, implicitly confirming that the fix is to *remove* the per-fragment global memory traffic (move depth on-chip), not to optimize the atomic itself. Our refutation that contention isn't the bottleneck aligns: the cost is the per-fragment global round-trip + compare, addressed by T5, not atomic tuning.
- **HW raster / hardware early-Z**: paper §3.4, §6.1 — the entire premise is that HW raster of micro-polys wastes ~4× on 2×2 quads; confirms our reason for SW raster and that we should NOT expand to quads unless derivatives are needed (their quad-expansion is opt-in, `RenderModeFlag_EnableQuads`).

## Honest portability caveats
- The **last-writer-wins shared-write trick** for ordering (no atomic) relies on documented-undefined-but-observed GF100 behavior; not safe to assume on Apple/Metal/WGSL. Use a workgroup-scoped `atomicMax` on the packed depth word instead (WGSL supports 32-bit workgroup atomics) — cheap, and our packed-24b-depth|8b-id scheme already fits a single `atomic<u32>`. This is the clean no-64-bit adaptation of their election.
- Their fixed-point math caps viewport at 2048² with 32-bit ints (`Constants.hpp`, README "Known issues"); our visbuffer already lives in 32-bit so this is a non-issue at our resolution but watch precision if tiling math reuses their S16 vertex packing.
- The bin/coarse two-stage split exists to bound queue-write fan-out from a single CTA. If we adopt T5 we likely need both stages (or an indirect-dispatch binning compute pass) to keep per-workgroup queue counts bounded — a real amount of new code.
