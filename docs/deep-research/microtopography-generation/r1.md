# Synthesising a 0.1 m Terrain Surface for Estonia from the 1 m Maa-amet DTM: A Generative-Model Architecture

## TL;DR
- Build a **pixel-space (not latent) conditional diffusion model that predicts only the high-frequency residual above a nearest-neighbour upsample of the 1 m DTM, and enforce the block-average downsampling constraint exactly via DDNM-style range-null-space projection**. This gives near-zero downsampling error by construction while letting the model invent all sub-metre detail. It is the single most important decision and it is a solved engineering problem.
- For the "beauty and variance" objective, the perception-distortion tradeoff (Blau & Michaeli, CVPR 2018) is the governing theory. They "prove mathematically that distortion and perceptual quality are at odds with each other... as the mean distortion decreases, this probability must increase (indicating worse perceptual quality)... this result holds true for any distortion measure, and is not only a problem of the PSNR or SSIM criteria." You correctly reject L2 fidelity and take the perceptual/stochastic end; diffusion and flow-matching samplers sit there and give per-tile diversity for free through the initial noise seed.
- Condition on the **10-25 cm Maa-amet orthophoto (RGB+NIR) plus a landform-class raster** via ControlNet/SPADE-style spatial conditioning; tile the country with **MultiDiffusion/InfiniteDiffusion overlapping-window fusion** and a deterministic per-tile hashed seed. The consistency constraint bounds cross-tile seams to the residual band, so seams are small and cheap to blend.

## Key Findings

### The core constraint is trivially and exactly satisfiable
Block-averaging from 0.1 m to 1 m is a linear operator A (each 1 m cell = mean of the 10x10 sub-cells). Its Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse A† is nearest-neighbour upsampling (replicate the coarse value into the block, with the appropriate scaling). The range-null decomposition x = A†Ax + (I - A†A)x means the coarse block-averages are pinned by the A†Ax term and the model only synthesises the null-space component (I - A†A)x, which is exactly the within-block detail that averages to zero. This makes A x̂ ≡ y hold identically. DDNM (Wang, Yu, Zhang, arXiv:2212.00490, ICLR 2023 Oral) is the canonical framework; it enforces "Consistency: A x̂ ≡ y, Realness: x̂ ∼ q(x)", and its official code (github.com/wyhuai/DDNM) ships an `sr_averagepooling` degradation mode, i.e. exactly your operator. The paper states it uses "the average-pooling downsampler as the degradation operator, which is easy to get the pseudo-inverse."

### Deep generative models are the right family, and here is the ranked shortlist
Diffusion/flow models dominate for the stated objective. Terrain-specific precedents already exist (Terrain Diffusion 2025; Lochner et al. CGF 2023; StyleDEM 2023). GANs remain viable and cheaper at inference but need mode-collapse mitigation. Latent diffusion is a trap here because the VAE breaks exact pixel consistency.

### Diversity is a free parameter you control
Stochastic samplers give one-to-many outputs; you inject controlled variance through seed, guidance scale, and style vectors, and keep reproducibility by hashing tile coordinates into the seed.

## Details

### THREAD A — Enforcing downsampling consistency (the hard constraint)

This is the decision everything else hangs off. You want A x̂ ≡ y where A is 10x10 block-averaging. Options, ranked:

**1. DDNM range-null-space projection (RECOMMENDED, training-free, EXACT).** DDNM (arXiv:2212.00490; open code) decomposes each denoised estimate x̂_0 at every reverse step as x̂_0 → A†y + (I - A†A)x̂_0. Because A† for block-averaging is nearest-neighbour upsampling (the paper's "easy pseudo-inverse" of average pooling), this is cheap (no SVD, no matrix inversion) and gives EXACT consistency: after projection the 1 m block-averages equal the source DTM to floating-point precision. DDNM's own SR mode uses average-pooling as A, so this is a documented, tested configuration. DDNM+ adds a time-travel trick and noise handling if you want to relax exactness to improve quality, but for a noise-free constraint use plain DDNM.

**2. Architectural guarantee via residual prediction (RECOMMENDED, combine with #1).** Train the network to output only the detail residual r = x - A†Ax (the high-frequency layer above the NN-upsampled coarse DTM). Add A†y back at the end. The coarse component is then exact by construction, independent of model quality. Terrain Diffusion (arXiv:2512.08309, Dec 2025) does essentially this with a Laplacian encoding that separates low- and high-frequency channels and re-extracts the low-frequency after synthesis. Guérin et al. and Argudo et al. also predict residual detail over an upsampled base. Combining residual prediction with a DDNM projection at sampling time is belt-and-braces: the residual keeps the network well-conditioned; the projection guarantees exactness even for the residual's own block-mean leakage.

**3. Hard alternating projection (subtract block-mean residual each step).** After every denoising step, compute the block-mean error and subtract it. This works but can push the iterate off the diffusion manifold, causing artifacts; DDNM is the principled version of the same idea because it only touches the range space and leaves the null-space (the generated detail) untouched. Prefer DDNM.

**4. DPS / ΠGDM / DDRM / RePaint / ILVR (approximate or special cases).** DPS (Chung et al., arXiv:2209.14687, ICLR 2023) uses a gradient of the measurement likelihood through the denoiser: general (handles nonlinear A) but only APPROXIMATELY consistent and needs a step-size hyperparameter. ΠGDM (Song et al., ICLR 2023) adds pseudo-inverse guidance, better but still approximate. DDRM (Kawar et al., NeurIPS 2022) denoises in the SVD spectral domain of A: exact-ish for linear A but needs the SVD, unnecessary overhead for block-averaging. DDNM's authors show ILVR, RePaint and DDRM guidance are special cases of range-null decomposition. For a clean linear block-average operator, DDNM strictly dominates DPS/ΠGDM on exactness and cost.

**5. Latent diffusion consistency (AVOID for the hard constraint).** With a Stable-Diffusion-style latent model the VAE encoder/decoder is nonlinear, so A acts in pixel space but the model works in latent space and you cannot project exactly. PSLD (Rout et al., NeurIPS 2023) and ReSample (Song et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv:2307.08123) enforce "hard data consistency" via inner optimisation loops, but they are approximate and add cost, and ReSample explicitly notes PSLD can leave artifacts under noise. Conclusion: **use a pixel-space diffusion model on the single-channel heightfield, not a latent model.** A single-channel 0.1 m heightfield at tile sizes of a few hundred px is entirely tractable in pixel space; you do not need the VAE compression that latent models buy you.

**Flow matching / rectified flow.** The same range-null logic transfers to flow-matching/rectified-flow samplers (the projection is on the predicted clean sample, independent of the forward process). OFTSR ("One-Step Flow for Image Super-Resolution with Tunable Fidelity-Realism Trade-offs," arXiv:2412.09465) is a flow-based SR model built explicitly around the Blau-Michaeli perception-distortion trade-off, giving a tunable fidelity-realism knob directly useful for dialing your perceptual-vs-consistency balance. Flow models also allow few-step distilled sampling (Terrain Diffusion uses consistency distillation), relevant if per-tile cost matters even offline.

### THREAD B — Generative terrain/heightfield synthesis and super-resolution

**Why L2 is forbidden and what to use instead.** Blau & Michaeli, "The Perception-Distortion Tradeoff", CVPR 2018 (arXiv:1711.06077, DOI 10.1109/CVPR.2018.00652) prove that any estimator minimising a distortion measure (MSE, PSNR, SSIM) must move away from the true data distribution, so it looks blurry and over-smooth ("regression to the mean"). Since your scoring function is perceptual quality and variance, you want the perceptual/distributional end, which is exactly what adversarial and diffusion objectives target. This paper is the theoretical justification for your whole approach; cite it front and centre.

**Diffusion for DEM/terrain (2023-2026).** Directly relevant:
- Terrain Diffusion (Goslin, arXiv:2512.08309, Dec 2025): hierarchical EDM2-backbone diffusion for infinite, seed-consistent, tileable terrain; InfiniteDiffusion algorithm (a MultiDiffusion successor) gives order-invariant constant-time random access; signed-square-root transform + Laplacian encoding for large dynamic range; few-step consistency distillation. This is the closest existing system to what you want and its tiling+seed machinery is directly reusable. Open-source infinite-tensor framework described.
- Lochner et al., "Interactive Authoring of Terrain using Diffusion Models", Computer Graphics Forum 42, e14941 (2023, DOI 10.1111/cgf.14941): conditional diffusion trained on real elevation, style selection + sketch control, resolution amplification.
- Broader DEM SR diffusion literature: SRDiff-style and remote-sensing SR (EDiffSR), "terrain feature-guided diffusion" void-filling, Schrödinger-bridge lunar topography (arXiv:2606.14638). These confirm diffusion transfers to single-channel elevation.

**GANs.** Guérin et al., "Interactive Example-Based Terrain Authoring with Conditional GANs", ACM TOG 36(6):228, SIGGRAPH Asia 2017 (DOI 10.1145/3130800.3130804, code+data released) is the seminal conditional-GAN terrain paper. Zhao et al., "Multi-theme generative adversarial terrain amplification", ACM TOG 38(6), 2019 (DOI 10.1145/3355089.3356553) does exactly amplification/super-resolution with multiple style themes. StyleDEM (Perche et al., arXiv:2304.09626, 2023) and "Authoring Terrains with Spatialised Style" (StyleGAN2-based) add spatially varying style control. ESRGAN/Real-ESRGAN can be applied to heightfields directly. GANs infer faster than diffusion but need mode-collapse mitigation (Thread C) and are harder to constrain exactly.

**Semantic-conditioned synthesis.** SPADE/GauGAN (Park, Liu, Wang, Zhu, CVPR 2019, arXiv:1903.07291, code released) is the reference for conditioning on a segmentation/landform-class raster via spatially-adaptive normalization, and natively supports multi-modal (diverse) synthesis via a style vector. Strong option if you go the GAN route conditioning on a landform-class map.

**Neural implicit / arbitrary-scale.** LIIF (Chen et al., CVPR 2021) and Local Texture Estimator (Lee & Jin, CVPR 2022) give continuous, arbitrary-scale decoding from a feature grid, which suits a coordinate-driven cluster-LOD renderer. Implicit Diffusion Models (Gao et al., CVPR 2023, arXiv:2303.16491) fuse an INR decoder with diffusion for continuous-resolution SR with diversity: the most on-target single paper for "continuous-resolution + generative + diverse". Downside: deterministic INR decoders (plain LIIF) regress to smooth output and will be blurry per Blau-Michaeli, so pair with adversarial/diffusion objectives.

**VQ / autoregressive / masked transformers.** VQGAN + MaskGIT/MUSE-style masked generative transformers give strong diversity and tileable sampling via masking schedules, but the VQ tokenizer, like the VAE, breaks exact pixel consistency. Same verdict as latent diffusion: avoid for the hard constraint unless you add a residual/projection correction in pixel space after decoding.

### THREAD C — Diversity, variance, stochasticity

- **Mode collapse in GANs:** if you go GAN, add MSGAN mode-seeking regularization (Mao et al., CVPR 2019, arXiv:1903.05628, code), which maximises output distance / latent distance, or DSGAN (Diversity-Sensitive cGAN, Yang et al., ICLR 2019). One-line additions to the generator loss.
- **Stochastic SR (one-to-many):** SRFlow (Lugmayr et al., ECCV 2020, arXiv:2006.14200, code) is a normalizing flow modelling the full conditional distribution. Its own repo lists as a feature: "Higher Consistency: When downsampling the super-resolution, one obtains almost the exact input," i.e. strong built-in consistency AND diversity via a temperature parameter. SR3 (Saharia et al., diffusion SR) and SRDiff give the same one-to-many property. Diffusion is the natural fit because your consistency method (DDNM) is already diffusion-based.
- **Injecting spatially varying variance:** vary the initial noise per region, use a per-tile style vector, and interpolate style vectors across the country grid so neighbouring tiles differ but transition smoothly. Classifier-free guidance scale is your global diversity knob: low guidance = more diverse, high guidance = tighter adherence to conditioning.
- **Determinism + variance together:** derive the diffusion seed from a hash of the tile's integer coordinates (seed = hash(tile_x, tile_y, dataset_version)). Fully reproducible bake with "different everywhere" richness. Terrain Diffusion's "seed consistency" property formalises exactly this.

### THREAD D — Seamless tiling at national scale

Estonia covers 45,339 km²; at 0.1 m that is ~4.53x10^12 cells, so tiling is mandatory.
- **MultiDiffusion (Bar-Tal et al., ICML 2023, arXiv:2302.08113, code in HuggingFace diffusers)** is the standard answer: run diffusion on many overlapping crops and, at each step, fuse per-crop predictions by averaging in overlap regions. Arbitrary-size seamless output from a fixed-size model with no retraining. **InfiniteDiffusion** (Terrain Diffusion, 2025) extends this to unbounded, order-invariant, constant-time random access, ideal for a country-scale bake regenerated in pieces.
- **The consistency constraint HELPS tiling.** Because A†y pins the coarse structure to the source DTM in every tile, adjacent tiles already agree on everything at >=1 m scale. Cross-tile discontinuities can only appear in the sub-metre residual, so seam magnitude is bounded by the residual amplitude (tens of cm at most). A real advantage over free-form image tiling.
- **Heightfield seams are worse than image seams:** a mismatch creates a visible geometric ridge and breaks normals/shading, so blend on the residual with overlap-and-feather or multi-band blending; Poisson blending on the residual works because the coarse field is already continuous. Avoid graph-cut seams for heightfields (they can create slope discontinuities).
- **Tiled VAE artifacts:** another reason to avoid latent models: tiled latent decoding produces well-known block artifacts at tile boundaries. Pixel-space + MultiDiffusion sidesteps this.

### THREAD E — Conditioning on semantics / landform class

Mechanisms, best to worst fit:
- **ControlNet (Zhang, Rao, Agrawala, ICCV 2023, arXiv:2302.05543, code+weights)** is the obvious way to condition a pretrained diffusion backbone on the NN-upsampled 1 m DTM AND a landform-class raster AND the orthophoto, as stacked conditioning channels, without disturbing the base model (zero-convolutions). Trains robustly on small datasets (the paper notes training is robust "with small (<50k) and large (>1m) datasets"). Recommended conditioning path.
- **SPADE** if GAN-based; **T2I-Adapter** as a lighter ControlNet alternative.
- **Classifier-free guidance scale** trades diversity vs adherence: keep it low-to-moderate to preserve your variance goal.

**The orthophoto is your highest-value conditioning signal.** The Maa-amet orthophoto is real, high-resolution, spatially registered imagery that already encodes furrow direction, field boundaries, vegetation texture, tree-throw mounds, and shading. Argudo, Chica, Andújar, "Terrain Super-resolution through Aerial Imagery and Fully Convolutional Networks", Computer Graphics Forum 37(2):101-110, 2018 (DOI 10.1111/cgf.13345) is the proof of concept: they drive plausible high-res DEM detail from orthophotos + low-res DEM. Monocular height estimation (IM2HEIGHT, Mou & Zhu, arXiv:1802.10249) shows an image alone carries strong height cues. Conditioning on RGB+NIR gives real, spatially varying structure even though resulting heights are not metrically true, which is exactly your goal.

**Estonian covariates available (all Maa-amet / national, open):**
- **Orthophoto:** nationwide RGB at 20-25 cm GSD, urban/low-altitude areas at 10-16 cm; also produced as false-colour CIR (Near Infrared, Red, Green) and forestry NGR. Free open data, captured on a 2-year rotating cycle (half of Estonia per year), CRS EPSG:3301. Sources: geoportaal.maaamet.ee orthophoto pages p309/p349/p662.
- **National airborne LiDAR:** LAZ 1.4, 1 km² tiles, currently 4+ pts/m² (older 2011 nationwide coverage ~2-3 pts/m²); DTM at 1/5/10/25 m, plus DSM, nDSM (1/5 m), CHM (4/10 m). Latest DEMs from 2021-2024 ALS. Free, no registration. Sources: geoportaal.maaamet.ee elevation pages p308/p664.
- **EstSoil-EH** (Kmoch, Kanal, Astover, Kull, Virro, Helm, Pärtel, Ostonen, Uuemaa, Earth System Science Data 13:83-97, 2021, DOI 10.5194/essd-13-83-2021; data Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.3473289): >20 eco-hydrological soil/topography/land-use variables synthesised from the Soil Map of Estonia (mullakaart) at 1:10,000 (>750,000 soil units, 75% smaller than 4 ha), the 5 m LiDAR DEM, ETAK, and EU-SoilHydroGrids.
- **Estonian Geological Survey (Eesti Geoloogiateenistus)** geological base map at 1:50,000 (bedrock, bedrock relief, Quaternary deposits + thickness, geomorphology), covering "more than 50%" of Estonia; older nationwide sets at 1:200,000 and 1:400,000. Sheets via egt.ee and Maa-amet geoportal.
- **ETAK** (Estonian Topographic Database): national vector land-cover/feature database, open data, feeds the 1:10,000 Basic Map. **Metsaregister** (forest register): stand inventory (species, age, volume, felling) maintained by Keskkonnaagentuur, exchanges data with ETAK. (Confirm Metsaregister's exact download/API terms directly before depending on it.)

**Estonian landform classes worth conditioning on:** peatland/bog (hummock-hollow, string-flark patterning), alvar/limestone pavement (clint-grike, karren), glacial till plain, drumlins/eskers/kames, forest floor (tree-throw pit-and-mound microrelief), agricultural fields (plough furrows, strongly directional), coastal, urban. Derive the class raster from soil + geology + ETAK + CHM.

### THREAD F — Training data / exemplars

Since the objective is beauty, train on ANY high-resolution terrain worldwide, preferring geomorphologically similar (low-relief, glaciated, peatland, till-plain, forested) regions:
- **Netherlands AHN4** (AHN.nl / OpenTopography DOI 10.5069/G9CN725M, CC0): 0.5 m raster DTM built from "a minimum of 10 height measurements per square meter," systematic and stochastic accuracy ±5 cm each, collected 2020-2022 over ~37,000 km² surveyed (Netherlands land area ~33,700 km²), datum Amersfoort/RD New (EPSG:28992) + NAP. Extremely dense and morphologically close to Estonia (low, glaciated, agricultural). Best single transfer source.
- **Finland (MML/NLS), Denmark (~8 pts/m², CC0), Sweden, northern Poland/Germany, Canadian Shield:** all glaciated low-relief analogues.
- **Switzerland swissSURFACE3D, UK EA 25cm-1m, USGS 3DEP QL0/QL1, Spain PNOA:** higher relief but useful for augmentation and rock/erosion texture.
- **Non-terrain displacement/material scans (Quixel Megascans, photogrammetric surface scans):** legitimate as a fine-detail texture prior for surfaces like forest floor and bare soil, since you are synthesising not reconstructing. Use as auxiliary training or as exemplar textures for patch-based/MPS priors.

**Transfer:** a diffusion model trained on AHN4 (and Finnish/Swedish/Danish LiDAR) should transfer to Estonia given geomorphological similarity; fine-tune on the sparse Estonian high-density LiDAR patches. Diffusion heightfield models train on patch crops, so tens of thousands of 256px crops (readily extracted from a few national datasets) are sufficient; augment with rotation/reflection (terrain has no canonical orientation except directional features like furrows, which you condition on rather than augment away).

### THREAD G — Evaluation for a perceptual objective

- **Hard-constraint metric (report always):** RMSE/MAE of downsample(synth) vs source 1 m DTM, in metres and as a fraction of the DTM's own vertical accuracy. With DDNM projection this should be ~1e-4 m (floating-point), effectively zero. This is your pass/fail gate.
- **Distributional/perceptual:** FID and KID between synth and real high-res terrain patch distributions (compute on hillshaded or multi-channel derivative renders, since FID on raw single-channel height is weak); SIFID (single-image FID, from SinGAN) for single-exemplar matching; LPIPS on renders.
- **Terrain-specific statistics:** radially averaged power spectral density / roughness spectrum (should follow the expected power law and match the exemplar band); variograms/semivariograms; slope and curvature distributions; hypsometry; Portilla-Simoncelli steerable-pyramid statistics for texture realism. The Perceived Terrain Realism Metric (Cordonnier/Guérin et al., ACM Transactions on Applied Perception 2022, geomorphon-based) is a purpose-built perceptual metric.
- **Diversity:** LPIPS-diversity across multiple samples from the same conditioning; per-pixel std across samples.
- **Rendering-oriented (critical, do not skip):** a heightfield can pass statistics but shimmer under rendering. Evaluate normal-map quality, ambient-occlusion/shading behaviour, silhouette at grazing angles, and LOD popping across your cluster hierarchy. High-frequency noise that looks fine in a heatmap can alias badly in-engine.

## Recommendations

**Recommended architecture (opinionated):**
1. **Backbone:** single-channel **pixel-space diffusion model** (EDM/EDM2 formulation, à la Karras; the backbone Terrain Diffusion uses) operating on 0.1 m residual heightfield patches (e.g. 256x256 = 25.6 m tiles or 512x512 = 51.2 m). NOT a latent/VAE model.
2. **Target parameterisation:** predict the **residual above nearest-neighbour upsample of the 1 m DTM**; add A†y back. Architectural half of the consistency guarantee.
3. **Consistency enforcement:** **DDNM range-null-space projection at every reverse step** with A = 10x10 average-pool, A† = NN-upsample. Exact, cheap, no SVD. Use DDNM (not DPS/ΠGDM). The belt-and-braces other half.
4. **Conditioning:** **ControlNet** stacking (a) NN-upsampled 1 m DTM + derivatives (slope, curvature), (b) landform-class raster from soil/geology/ETAK/CHM, (c) 10-25 cm RGB+NIR orthophoto. Orthophoto is the highest-value channel.
5. **Diversity:** stochastic sampling with per-tile seed = hash(tile_x, tile_y, version); moderate classifier-free guidance; optional style vector interpolated across the national grid.
6. **Tiling:** **MultiDiffusion/InfiniteDiffusion** overlapping-window fusion; blend residuals with feathered overlap or multi-band blending; rely on the coarse pin to bound seams.
7. **Training data:** pre-train on **Netherlands AHN4** + Finnish/Swedish/Danish LiDAR crops (glaciated low-relief analogues); fine-tune on Estonian high-density LiDAR patches; augment with rotation/reflection; optional Megascans micro-detail prior.
8. **Evaluation suite:** downsampling RMSE (gate) + FID/KID/SIFID on renders + PSD/variogram/slope/curvature matching + LPIPS-diversity + in-engine normal/AO/silhouette/LOD-popping check.

**Staged plan:**
- **Stage 1 (validate the constraint):** implement DDNM average-pool projection on an off-the-shelf pixel diffusion model, on a single 256px Estonian tile. Benchmark: downsampling RMSE < 1 mm. If not met, the projection is buggy; fix before anything else.
- **Stage 2 (get detail that looks good):** train/fine-tune the residual diffusion model on AHN4. Benchmark: FID on hillshade beats bicubic and beats a plain L2 baseline; PSD slope within the exemplar band.
- **Stage 3 (condition it):** add ControlNet with orthophoto + landform class. Benchmark: bogs/fields/forest visibly differ and match their class exemplars; furrow direction follows the orthophoto.
- **Stage 4 (scale + seam):** MultiDiffusion tiling + hashed seeds over a multi-tile region. Benchmark: no visible seam ridges in-engine; deterministic re-bake reproduces bit-identical output.
- **Stage 5 (diversity tuning):** tune guidance/seed/style for target LPIPS-diversity without losing class adherence.

**Thresholds that change the plan:**
- If downsampling RMSE cannot be driven near zero with residual+DDNM, you have an operator-definition bug (block alignment/scaling), not a model problem.
- If FID gains from diffusion over a cheap GAN are marginal on YOUR terrain, switch to a SPADE/StyleDEM GAN + MSGAN reg for far cheaper inference; the consistency projection still applies post-hoc.
- If per-tile diffusion cost is unacceptable even offline, distill to a few-step consistency/flow model (Terrain Diffusion does this).

## Caveats
- **Solved vs open.** Exact consistency for a linear block-average operator is SOLVED (DDNM + residual). Seamless tiling is SOLVED (MultiDiffusion/InfiniteDiffusion). What is genuinely OPEN/empirical: (a) how well a model trained on AHN4/Nordic LiDAR transfers to Estonian microtopography without visible domain artifacts; (b) how to build a clean landform-class raster from the Estonian soil/geology/forest layers; (c) tuning the diversity-vs-class-adherence balance; (d) preventing render-time shimmer from synthesised high-frequency detail. Budget most of your research time here, not on the constraint.
- **Terrain Diffusion (arXiv:2512.08309)** is very recent (Dec 2025) and its infinite-tensor framework maturity is unverified; treat its InfiniteDiffusion as a design to reimplement/validate, not a turnkey dependency.
- **Latent/VQ models** are attractive for their priors and speed but fundamentally compromise exact pixel consistency and add tiling artifacts. If you must use a pretrained latent backbone for its rich prior, apply a final pixel-space residual + DDNM correction pass to restore the constraint, and expect some quality cost.
- The block-averaging pseudo-inverse scaling convention (1/s vs 1/s²) is standard linear algebra but should be verified against DDNM's Sec. 3.2 and the `sr_averagepooling` code path for your exact 10x scale.
- Metsaregister open-data/download terms should be confirmed directly before you depend on it.
- FID/KID on heightfields are imperfect; always corroborate with terrain statistics and in-engine rendering, which is your true objective.
