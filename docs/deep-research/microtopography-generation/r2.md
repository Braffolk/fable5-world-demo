# Reconstructing Decimeter-Scale Terrain from 1 m DEMs: A Technical Assessment for the `laas` Project

## TL;DR
- Going from 1 m to 0.1 m is NOT information recovery below roughly 2x-4x; beyond that, any decimeter detail is synthesised, not reconstructed, because the information never existed in the 1 m grid (Estonian LiDAR is only 2.1 pts/m² rural, so a 1 m grid is already near its true resolving limit). Your goal of "physically plausible, statistically grounded" detail is the correct and achievable framing; "1:1 reconstruction" at 0.1 m is not.
- The strongest fit for `laas` is a hybrid: (1) a deterministic, conditioned generative model (conditional GAN or sparse dictionary) that adds landform-class-specific microtexture keyed to Estonian covariates (bog, alvar, till, klint), combined with (2) process-based amplifiers for the few landforms with genuine, cheap physics (peatland hummock-hollow, karst grikes, patterned ground). Deterministic, tileable, seed-reproducible synthesis is essential for a Nanite-style streamed cluster pipeline.
- Conditioning on geology/soil is well-supported by the SCORPAN digital-soil-mapping literature and empirical soil-roughness statistics (RMS height, correlation length by tillage/texture) which give you the actual numbers to parameterise per-class microtexture. Estonian raw LiDAR point clouds, 10-20 cm orthophotos, and Eesti Geoloogiateenistus bedrock/Quaternary + soil maps are the covariate stack.

## Key Findings

### 1. The information limit is real and quantifiable
Super-resolution of a DEM below its sampling resolution is mathematically ill-posed: the fine-scale variance was never sampled. Panangian & Bittner (2024, Real-GDSR, ISPRS Annals X-2-2024, 185-192, DOI 10.5194/isprs-annals-X-2-2024-185-2024, DLR) state that real low-resolution DSMs "do not preserve as much information" as bicubic-downsampled synthetic ones, which is why models trained on synthetic degradation fail on real data. Error grows sharply with upscaling factor: the GSRMTL multitask model (Int. J. Appl. Earth Obs. Geoinf.) reports RMSE of 1.63 m, 2.23 m and 3.27 m at x4, x8 and x16 respectively, and explicitly notes "the performance gap widens as the scale factor increases... where error accumulation is more pronounced." Lai et al. (2017, LapSRN, CVPR, arXiv:1704.03915) is blunt: their network "does not 'hallucinate' fine details" and "is not able to hallucinate details if the LR input image does not consist of sufficient amount of structure."

Practical implication for you: 1 m -> 0.1 m is a 10x linear (100x areal) upscaling. That is far beyond the ~2-4x regime where detail is genuinely recoverable. Everything below roughly 0.25-0.5 m is invention. The honest design target is therefore synthesis that (a) is statistically indistinguishable from real decimeter terrain of that landform class, and (b) reproduces the 1 m input when downsampled (the "downsampling consistency" or "adjustment" constraint).

### 2. Downsampling consistency is a solved constraint you should adopt
Metzger et al. (2023) use an "adjustment step" in a guided-diffusion DSM super-resolution that constrains the output "to always match the source image when downsampled." Real-GDSR (Panangian & Bittner 2024) deliberately drops it because their real LR input is too information-poor, but for you the constraint is exactly right: it guarantees your synthesised 0.1 m clusters aggregate back to the authoritative Maa-amet 1 m DTM, so your renderer never drifts from ground truth at coarse LODs. Implement it as a hard projection (subtract the block-mean residual) after any generative step.

### 3. DEM super-resolution / neural terrain families
- **CNN residual/feedback nets.** D-SRCNN (Demiray et al. 2020), DSRFB/AFN feedback network (Kubade et al. 2020, arXiv:2007.01940 and 2010.01626) - deterministic, fast, but produce smoothed output that under-represents high-frequency roughness. Good as a trend predictor, poor as a microtexture source.
- **Terrain-feature-aware nets.** TfaSR (Zhang, Yu & Zhu 2022, ISPRS J. Photogramm. 189:143; code at Figshare DOI 10.6084/m9.figshare.19597201) uses deformable convolutions and explicit terrain-feature loss. Reports RMSE improvements of 7.8-53.7% over bicubic at 30m->10m. Deterministic. Only demonstrated at coarse scales.
- **GANs.** D-SRGAN (Demiray et al. 2021, ESRGAN for DSM); Guérin et al. (2017) conditional GAN terrain authoring (ACM TOG 36(6):228, DOI 10.1145/3130800.3130804, code + data released, reimplementations e.g. nanoxas/sketch-to-terrain). GANs add convincing high-frequency detail but "may also introduce pseudo-details that do not match real terrain." Stochastic unless you fix the latent/noise seed.
- **Diffusion / implicit.** Implicit Diffusion Model (Gao et al. 2023, CVPR, arXiv:2303.16491) gives continuous-resolution SR via an implicit neural representation + DDPM - directly relevant because it decouples output resolution from a fixed upscaling factor. CDEM (Yao et al. 2024) uses implicit neural representations to predict elevation at arbitrary coordinates (relevant to a coordinate-driven cluster renderer) but "assumes regular, grid-like structures." Multi-modal DEM SR using relative depth (2025, ScienceDirect S1569843225005126) fuses optical imagery via Depth Anything. Diffusion is stochastic and slow at inference (hundreds of steps) - a poor fit for real-time unless distilled/precomputed.
- **StyleDEM** (GAN for terrain synthesis/authoring with interactive brushes, style manipulation and super-resolution) is the closest to an authoring toolbox.

### 4. Graphics-side terrain amplification (best conceptual fit)
This literature explicitly targets plausible, not accurate, detail - your exact framing.
- **Guérin, Digne, Galin, Peytavie (2016), "Sparse representation of terrains for procedural modeling"** (Computer Graphics Forum 35(2), Eurographics). Dictionary/sparse-coding amplification. Matlab code on GitHub (eric-guerin/terrain-amplification). Deterministic given dictionary + input. Example-based: learns atoms from real high-res exemplars.
- **Argudo, Andujar, Chica, Guérin et al. (2017)** (The Visual Computer 33(6):1005, DOI 10.1007/s00371-017-1393-6) - fully convolutional nets upsampling DEMs using orthophotos as guidance, "up to one order of magnitude more resolution." Directly relevant: you have 10-20 cm Estonian orthophotos.
- **Guérin et al. (2017) cGAN authoring**; **Zhao et al. (2019) Multi-theme GAN terrain amplification** (ACM TOG 38(6)); **Schott, Paris, Fournier, Guérin, Galin (2023)** large-scale terrain authoring via interactive erosion (ACM TOG 42); **Grenier et al. (2024)** real-time terrain enhancement with controlled procedural patterns (CGF, DOI 10.1111/cgf.14992).
- **Process-based synthesis (Cordonnier et al.):** Large Scale Terrain Generation from Tectonic Uplift and Fluvial Erosion (2016, CGF 35(2)); Sculpting Mountains: Interactive Terrain Modeling Based on Subsurface Geology (2018, IEEE TVCG 24(5):1756, DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2017.2689022) - directly conditions terrain on stratigraphy/subsurface layers; Forming Terrains by Glacial Erosion (2023, ACM TOG 42(4), source code available); Tzathas, Gailleton, Steer & Cordonnier (2024) physically-based analytical erosion (CGF, DOI 10.1111/cgf.15033). These are mostly mountain-scale; the fluvial/erosion machinery is largely irrelevant to low-relief Estonia, but the stratigraphy-conditioning idea transfers directly.

### 5. Geostatistical downscaling with covariates
Area-to-point regression kriging (ATPRK; Wang, Shi, Atkinson, Zhao 2015, Remote Sensing of Environment) and geographically-weighted ATPRK (2018, Remote Sensing 10(4):579, DOI 10.3390/rs10040579) downscale a coarse field using fine covariates while preserving the coarse aggregate (mass-preserving, i.e. downsampling-consistent by construction). The `dissever` GAM approach does the same with nonlinear covariate functions. These are deterministic and honest about uncertainty but produce smooth trend, not roughness. Conditional simulation (sequential Gaussian simulation, turning bands) adds stochastic roughness with the correct variogram, but is stochastic - you would need to fix the RNG per cluster for reproducibility.

### 6. Fractal scaling: partly valid, dangerous naively
Self-affine (fractal) scaling of terrain roughness holds over many decades in many settings (Science Advances, Gujrati et al. 2019, DOI 10.1126/sciadv.aax0847, "observed from the atomic to the tectonic scale"). BUT: RMS height "is not scale invariant" and roughness parameters "often change with the length of the profile" (soil-roughness radar literature). Real terrain has characteristic scale breaks: soil-roughness studies find distinct dual-scale processes (seedbed rows vs wheel tracks) with different RMS/correlation length per scale (Marzahn et al.). So a single Hurst exponent from 1 m data will NOT correctly predict 0.1 m roughness - the fractal dimension itself changes across the scale break. Naive midpoint displacement is physically wrong at cm-dm scales precisely because it assumes one exponent. Correct approach: fit band-limited self-affine statistics (RMS height + correlation length + roughness exponent) per landform class from real sub-decimeter exemplars, and synthesise only within the band where scaling actually holds.

### 7. Conditioning on geology/soil: strong empirical basis
- **SCORPAN framework** (McBratney, Mendonça Santos, Minasny 2003, Geoderma 117:3, DOI 10.1016/S0016-7061(03)00223-4): soil = f(soil, climate, organisms, relief, parent material, age, space). Parent material/lithology (p) is an established covariate; adding it raised soil-class prediction accuracy from ~45-66% to ~60-74% in one study (Bogor, Indonesia, Geoderma Regional 2023). Surface roughness "can be an indicator for changing parent material conditions" (hyper-scale DSM, Behrens et al., Geoderma 2014).
- **Empirical soil-roughness statistics** give you the actual per-class numbers. Agricultural tillage studies: smooth harrowed/rolled surfaces have RMS height <=1 cm at 1 m; ploughed ~3.5 cm; a TLS study reports ploughed 2.49 cm vs harrowed 1.5 cm RMS height, with correlation length more affected by tillage than RMS height (up to 2.6x). Roughness decomposes into a "dual scale process" with different statistics per scale. These map directly to per-landcover microtexture parameters.
- **Rock outcrop roughness from lithology:** SfM machine-learning study (Springer, Earth Science Informatics 2024, DOI 10.1007/s12145-024-01280-z) predicts millimetre-scale outcrop ruggedness from drone imagery (HSV + terrain ruggedness index). SfM sub-mm DEMs of rock breakdown (Earth Surface Dynamics) achieve ~0.3 mm vertical error - ground truth for lithology-conditioned roughness.

### 8. Estonia-specific: the landforms and the data
Estonian terrain is glaciated low-relief: drumlins/megaflutings, eskers and kame fields (Pandivere Upland radial eskers), till plains, the North Estonian Klint (Cambrian-Ordovician sandstones/limestones), alvars (thin soil over Ordovician-Silurian limestone/dolomite karst), and extensive peatlands. Per Orru & Orru (Estonian Journal of Earth Sciences 2008), "Estonia is rich in peatlands, which cover 22.3% of its territory" (corroborated by loodusveeb.ee: "about 22% of the Estonian land areas"). Per "Geology and Mineral Resources of Estonia" (geoloogia.info), Estonian drumlins "including megaflutings...may reach 13 km in length, and 80 m in height (Saadjärv Drumlin Field)" (a related source gives the Laiuse drumlin as 13 km long, 60 m high, and Vooremaa drumlins generally 2-13 km long, up to 70 m). Each landform has characteristic sub-meter microtopography:

- **Peatland hummock-hollow / string-flark.** Best-characterised sub-meter structure. Per Moore, Lukenbach, Thompson, Kettridge, Granath & Waddington (2019, Biogeosciences 16:3491-3506, DOI 10.5194/bg-16-3491-2019), "Hummocks and hollows occur at a spatial scale of 1 to 10 m...The hummock surface is typically located ~0.20 m or higher above the water table"; Korpela et al. (2020, Mires and Peat 26, art. 03) map microforms with a hummock-index threshold of 0.2 m. Process/statistical models exist: HuHoLa (Ecological Modelling 2025, S0304380025001978) classifies hummock-hollow-lawn from a DEM alone via sink-filling; HOHUM (Nungesser 2003) is a process model. String-flark aapa-mire patterning is oriented perpendicular to water flow, strings generally <1 m tall. Estonian anchor: Lode & Leivits (2011, Estonian J. Earth Sci. 60(4):232-248, DOI 10.3176/earth.2011.4.04) found 1 m and 2 m DTMs "fitted well for visualization of mire micro-topography" while 5-10 m only captured landscape scale.
- **Alvar/karst.** Limestone pavement clint-grike systems: clints typically 0.4-2.8 m², grikes usually <2 m deep and 3.2-6.5 cm wide (Burren analogue). Karren (rillenkarren, kamenitzas, solution hollows 5-30 cm wide). Deterministic given a joint/fracture network - synthesise grikes along a fracture pattern conditioned on bedding orientation. Telbisz et al. (2024, Remote Sensing 16(5):737, DOI 10.3390/rs16050737) show LiDAR doline density 25% higher than from topographic maps.
- **Glacial till / patterned ground.** Earth hummocks average 80-160 cm diameter, 40-60 cm height, on 58-99% clay/silt materials (frost heave/cryoturbation). Sorted stone polygons 2-3 m diameter. These are conditioned on soil texture + moisture + frost - parameterisable per soil-map unit.
- **Estonian LiDAR data.** RIEGL Newsroom (25 Jan 2021): the Estonian Land Board's VQ-1560i "has enabled to cover the whole country with 2.1 p/m2 elevation data. In urban areas the point density is even higher being at 18 p/m2." A new RIEGL VQ-1460 (with IGI UrbanMapper 2 EVO camera) was installed in 2024, with initial flights in spring 2025. DTM/DSM products at 1, 5, 10, 25 m; latest from 2021-2024 ALS; EPSG:3301 (L-EST97), EH2000 heights; free open data, LAZ 1.4 point clouds per 1 km² tile. University of Tartu work uses this DTM for fine feature extraction: Virro, Kmoch, Lidberg, Murua, Chan, Moges & Uuemaa (2025, Big Earth Data 9(2):243-264, DOI 10.1080/20964471.2025.2491177) detect drainage ditches via U-Net + transfer learning, identifying model FT3A (F1 = 0.766) "as the most effective model for detecting ditches in Estonia."

### 9. Rendering fit (Nanite-style)
Nanite-style cluster-LOD streaming needs detail that is (a) deterministic and seed-reproducible per cluster (so a re-streamed cluster is bit-identical), (b) locally generable without global state (so clusters can be built on demand), and (c) consistent across LOD levels. This strongly favours sparse-dictionary amplification (Guérin 2016) and fixed-seed procedural/conditional-GAN synthesis over stochastic diffusion or unconstrained conditional simulation. Any generative step must be followed by the downsampling-consistency projection so LOD levels agree.

## Details

### Method-by-method scorecard
For each family: (a) key papers, (b) code, (c) real-time/Nanite fit, (d) geology/soil conditioning, (e) deterministic vs stochastic.

**Sparse-dictionary amplification (Guérin et al. 2016).** (a) CGF 35(2). (b) Matlab code public. (c) Excellent - local, patch-based, fast, tileable. (d) Yes, via per-class dictionaries (train one dictionary per Estonian landform). (e) Deterministic given dictionary + input. Recommended as the backbone.

**Orthophoto-guided FCN (Argudo et al. 2017).** (a) Visual Computer 33(6). (b) Partial. (c) Good - feed-forward. (d) Indirect (orthophoto encodes land cover). (e) Deterministic. You have 10-20 cm orthophotos, so this is immediately actionable.

**Conditional GAN authoring (Guérin et al. 2017; StyleDEM).** (a) ACM TOG 36(6). (b) Code + data released; community reimplementations. (c) Good if you fix the noise seed and run feed-forward. (d) Yes - condition on sketched/rasterised feature maps derived from geology/soil layers. (e) Stochastic by default, deterministic with fixed seed.

**Diffusion / implicit (IDM 2023; CDEM 2024; Metzger 2023).** (a) arXiv:2303.16491 etc. (b) Some public. (c) Poor for real-time (multi-step sampling); usable only if precomputed offline and cached, or distilled. (d) Yes via conditioning. (e) Stochastic; IDM gives continuous resolution and CDEM gives coordinate queries, both attractive architecturally. Use the adjustment step (Metzger) for consistency.

**ATPRK / GWATPRK geostatistics.** (a) RSE 2015; RS 10(4):579. (b) R (partly). (c) Offline precompute only. (d) Yes - covariates are the whole point. (e) Deterministic (kriging) for the trend; add conditional simulation for stochastic roughness. Mass-preserving = downsampling-consistent for free.

**Process-based (Cordonnier subsurface/glacial; HuHoLa; HOHUM; frost-heave models).** (a) IEEE TVCG 24(5); ACM TOG 42(4); Ecological Modelling 2025. (b) Some (glacial erosion source released; HuHoLa described). (c) Offline bake to displacement/normal maps per landform, then stream. (d) Native - these ARE the geology/process conditioning. (e) Deterministic (given parameters/seed).

### What I would actually build
1. Segment Estonia into landform/microtexture classes using the covariate stack: Maa-amet 1 m DTM derivatives (slope, TPI, wetness), Eesti Geoloogiateenistus bedrock + Quaternary maps, the soil map (mullakaart), Metsaregister land cover, and 10-20 cm orthophotos. This is the SCORPAN state-space, used in reverse to predict a roughness class rather than a soil property.
2. For each class, estimate band-limited roughness statistics (RMS height, correlation length, roughness exponent) from real sub-decimeter exemplars: your own drone-SfM captures over representative Estonian bog/alvar/till sites (SfM gives mm-cm vertical accuracy), plus published values (tillage/soil-roughness tables, peatland hummock ~0.2 m, earth hummock 40-60 cm).
3. Deterministic synthesis per class: sparse-dictionary amplification (Guérin 2016) or fixed-seed conditional GAN, followed by the downsampling-consistency projection against the 1 m DTM.
4. Process-based overrides where physics is strong and cheap: HuHoLa-style hummock-hollow fields for bogs, fracture-network grike synthesis for alvars, frost-heave hummock placement for clay-rich till.
5. Bake to displacement + normal maps per cluster; stream via your Nanite-style LOD hierarchy.

## Recommendations
1. **Reframe the target now.** Commit internally to "physically-plausible synthesised decimeter detail, downsampling-consistent with the 1 m DTM," not "1:1 reconstruction." The information-theoretic argument (Lai et al. 2017; Panangian & Bittner 2024) makes the latter impossible below ~0.25-0.5 m. This changes validation from per-pixel RMSE to distributional metrics (roughness spectra, variograms, RMS height/correlation length matching real exemplars).
2. **Start with sparse-dictionary amplification + orthophoto guidance** (Guérin 2016; Argudo 2017). Deterministic, fast, Nanite-friendly, code exists. Benchmark against bicubic and against held-out drone-SfM ground truth.
3. **Build the Estonian covariate stack and per-class roughness table** before any ML. This is the highest-value, lowest-risk work and it is what makes the synthesis "grounded."
4. **Add process-based amplifiers only for bog, alvar, and patterned till** - the three landforms with strong, cheap, well-characterised sub-meter physics. Skip fluvial/glacial-erosion LEMs; Estonia's relief is too low for them to matter at 0.1 m.
5. **Capture your own exemplars.** A few drone-SfM or TLS surveys of representative bog/alvar/till/klint sites (sub-decimeter) are worth more than any amount of borrowed mountain data. They serve as training data, validation ground truth, and dictionary atoms simultaneously.
6. **Always apply the downsampling-consistency projection.** Non-negotiable for LOD agreement in a streamed renderer.

**Thresholds that change the plan:** if held-out SfM shows your synthesis matches real roughness spectra within a class, scale it up. If a landform class shows no stable band-limited scaling (roughness statistics keep drifting with window size), do not synthesise it fractally - fall back to exemplar tiling or a process model. If diffusion distillation gets inference under a few ms per cluster, revisit diffusion for its superior detail realism.

## Caveats
- Below ~0.25-0.5 m everything is invention. No method changes this; they only change how plausible the invention is.
- Fractal/self-affine synthesis is physically wrong if you assume a single exponent across the cm-m scale break. Always band-limit and re-fit per class.
- Most graphics terrain-amplification papers target high-relief mountains; their fluvial/tectonic machinery does not transfer to low-relief Estonia, though their example-based and conditioning frameworks do.
- Diffusion/implicit models give the best detail realism but are stochastic and slow; they need seed-fixing and precomputation to fit a real-time streamed renderer.
- Some cited figures (drumlin dimensions, hummock sizes, grike widths) are from regional analogues (Burren, Canadian Arctic, Fennoscandia) not Estonia specifically; validate against Estonian exemplars before trusting them numerically.
- The GSRMTL x4/x8/x16 RMSE figures and the CDEM/Metzger details come partly from secondary citations within other papers; confirm against primary sources before quoting them in any publication.
