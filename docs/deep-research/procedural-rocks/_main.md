# Procedural Generation of High-Fidelity 3D Rocks: Mesh, Texture, and End-to-End Pipelines Portable to WebGPU/JS

## TL;DR
- **Best mesh-only path:** compose rock geometry as an SDF (Inigo Quilez fBM-SDF sphere-grid + Worley F2−F1 cellular cracking + strata bands + domain warp), then mesh it once on the GPU. Use **Dual Contouring / Manifold Dual Contouring** when you need sharp fracture edges and **GPU Marching Cubes** (Will Usher's WGSL implementation performs on par with native Vulkan) or Naive Surface Nets for smooth weathered forms. For angular boulders and gravel, add **Voronoi/Delaunay fracture** (Raghavachary 2002) computed on the GPU via the **Jump Flooding Algorithm** (Rong & Tan 2006, log₂n passes).
- **Best texture-only path:** reimplement the **Substance 3D Designer rock graph vocabulary** (cellular/Worley noise → slope blur → directional/non-directional warp → flood fill → height blend → curvature/AO derived from height) as a chain of WGSL compute passes, baking albedo/normal/roughness/height/AO to real texture maps. Inigo Quilez's analytic noise/fbm/voronoi/domain-warp articles supply drop-in GPU code; triplanar mapping with Whiteout/RNM normal blending (Ben Golus) applies them to UV-free rock geometry.
- **Best both-together path:** the SDF→mesh→derived-maps blueprint is the strongest fit — the SAME field that produces the geometry gives you height, cavity, curvature and AO maps for free. Production Houdini generators (Skidmore/Gunfire, Kurzemnieks, kjohnsoninfo) prove the recipe (VDB SDF → Worley F2−F1 → strata slicing → edge chipping → triplanar PBR) but must be reimplemented; no drop-in WebGPU rock generator of production quality exists yet.

## Key Findings
1. **The field-then-mesh paradigm is the throughline.** Nearly every high-fidelity, portable technique builds a scalar/signed-distance field and extracts a triangle mesh once. This satisfies the "asset not shader" constraint perfectly and lets a Nanite-style pipeline consume dense meshes. Meshing choice controls fidelity: Marching Cubes/Surface Nets round sharp edges; Dual Contouring preserves them via QEF but can be non-manifold; Manifold Dual Contouring fixes that.
2. **Sharp vs. smooth is a meshing decision, not a field decision.** The same SDF meshed with Naive Surface Nets gives rounded, weathered pebbles/boulders; meshed with Dual Contouring gives crisp fractured/angular stone. Pick the mesher per rock archetype.
3. **Voronoi fracture is the canonical method for angular stone**, and it parallelizes on the GPU cleanly via jump flooding. Raghavachary (2002) is the seminal reference; Schvartzman & Otaduy generalize to high-dimensional CVD.
4. **Texture generation is a portable node-graph problem.** Substance Designer rock graphs are just deterministic image-processing passes (noise, blur, warp, blend, flood fill) — all trivially expressible as WGSL compute. This is the single most directly-reimplementable texture bucket.
5. **Both-together is nearly free when you start from an SDF**, because height/curvature/cavity/AO maps derive from the same field and its gradient. Production Houdini tools confirm the visual quality target and the exact node recipe, even though their VEX/VDB implementations must be ported.
6. **The blobby-result trap is real.** Convex-hull-of-noisy-points and simple sphere-displacement approaches (e.g. gl-rock) produce low-quality rounded blobs and should be treated as wiring references, not quality targets.
7. **Neural approaches are out of scope** — single-image SVBRDF capture, diffusion PBR/3D generators cannot be reimplemented as pure WGSL/JS and require heavy trained inference runtimes.

## Details

### CATEGORY 1 — MESH-ONLY GENERATION

#### 1a. SDF / implicit field composition, meshed once (STRONGEST recommendation)
**Technique.** Build the rock as a signed distance field, then run an isosurface extractor to bake a triangle mesh. Core field building blocks, all analytic and GPU-friendly:
- **Inigo Quilez fBM-SDF** (`iquilezles.org/articles/fbmsdf/`): builds fractal-noise displacement that *remains a valid SDF* by iteratively combining an infinite grid of random-radius spheres with smooth-min across octaves, instead of adding noise (which breaks the SDF gradient). In IQ's words, it uses "a grid of spheres of random sizes… You can combine different octaves with (smooth)minimum so FBM structures remain an SDF" (Shadertoy Ws3XWl). This is the ideal base for organic rock lumpiness.
- **Worley / Voronoi F2−F1** cellular noise for cracking and blocky cell structure (the exact noise Skidmore identifies as "one of the best for blocky, cellular shapes").
- **Strata/stratification bands** (sinusoidal or stepped displacement along one axis) for sedimentary layering.
- **Domain warping** (IQ) to break regularity.
- **Smooth min/max** to union sphere-grid SDFs into larger forms.

**Output.** A triangle mesh after extraction. Density is a function of grid resolution — hundreds of thousands to millions of triangles are trivially achievable (desirable here).

**Meshing methods and fidelity tradeoff (critical):**
- **GPU Marching Cubes** — Will Usher's WebGPU/WGSL implementation (`github.com/Twinklebear/webgpu-marching-cubes`, blog `willusher.io/graphics/2024/04/22/webgpu-marching-cubes/`) is data-parallel, uses a work-efficient parallel prefix scan (GPU Gems 3 Harris et al. translated directly to WGSL), and "performs on par with the native Vulkan one." This is a ready-to-adapt WGSL mesher. Rounds sharp features.
- **Naive Surface Nets** — bonsairobo/fast-surface-nets-rs (Rust) generates, per its README, "about 20 million triangles per second on a single core of a 2.5 GHz Intel Core i7," achieved via "small lookup tables and SIMD acceleration provided by glam." Per the same docs it "is nearly the same as Dual Contouring, but instead of using hermite (derivative) data to estimate surface points, Surface Nets will do a simpler form of interpolation (average) between points where the isosurface crosses voxel cube edges." Smooth output, rounds sharp corners. Ideal for weathered pebbles/boulders. Algorithm is compact and portable to JS/WGSL; explicitly chunk-friendly for large volumes.
- **Dual Contouring** — Ju, Losasso, Schaefer, Warren, "Dual Contouring of Hermite Data" (SIGGRAPH 2002 / ACM TOG 21(3):339–346, DOI 10.1145/566654.566586). Uses Hermite data (exact edge intersection points + normals) and a numerically stable QEF (quadratic error function) to place one dual vertex per cell, reproducing sharp edges/corners that Marching Cubes rounds off. The abstract describes "a new, numerically stable representation for quadratic error functions." Can produce non-manifold vertices/edges.
- **Manifold Dual Contouring** — Schaefer, Ju, Warren (IEEE TVCG 13(3):610–619, 2007, DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2007.1012). Adds an "octree-based topology-preserving vertex-clustering algorithm" so the contoured surface "contains only manifold vertices and edges, preserves sharp features." Use this when you need both crisp fracture edges AND watertight manifold geometry.
- **sdf2mesh** (WilstonOreo, `github.com/WilstonOreo/sdf2mesh`) — Rust + wgpu, converts an SDF (WGSL or GLSL shader) to a triangle mesh via dual contouring, slice-by-slice to save memory. Directly relevant: it already runs SDF→DC→mesh on wgpu. Caveats from the author: mesh size grows O(n³) with resolution (a 2048³ mesh can be several GB STL); DC "still has some problems with certain triangle constellations and precision," occasionally creating holes at high resolution — post-process to fix.

**Reimplementation / GPU notes.** Field evaluation is embarrassingly parallel (one thread per voxel). Marching Cubes needs a two-pass parallel scan for output compaction (Usher shows the WGSL). DC/MDC need per-cell QEF solves (small 3×3 normal equations or SVD) — heavier but still parallelizable. All portable to WGSL compute or JS.

**Performance.** Interactive to near-interactive for moderate grids; Usher's WGSL MC matches native Vulkan. Memory is the constraint at very high resolution (O(n³)); use chunking (surface nets is explicitly chunk-friendly) or sparse/octree fields.

**Fidelity.** Highest of any portable approach when paired with good field composition. This is the recommended core of a rock generator.

#### 1b. Voronoi / Delaunay fracture (best for angular boulders, shards, gravel)
**Technique.** Scatter seed points, compute Voronoi cells, clip the input volume by each cell's half-space intersection to yield convex fragments. **Raghavachary, "Fracture generation on polygonal meshes using Voronoi polygons"** (SIGGRAPH 2002 sketch, DOI 10.1145/1242073.1242200): scatter Voronoi sites across the mesh, compute the diagram, interpret Voronoi boundaries as cracks (or track a crack through the network). **Schvartzman & Otaduy, "Fracture animation based on high-dimensional Voronoi diagrams"** (I3D 2014, DOI 10.1145/2556700.2556713) generalize to a high-dimensional centroidal Voronoi diagram guided by the deformation field. Production analogues: Blender Cell Fracture add-on, Houdini Voronoi Fracture.

**GPU parallelization — Jump Flooding Algorithm.** Rong & Tan, "Jump Flooding in GPU with Applications to Voronoi Diagram and Distance Transform" (I3D 2006, DOI 10.1145/1111411.1111431). Computes an approximate Voronoi diagram / distance transform in log₂(n) passes (step lengths n/2, n/4, …, 1) over an n×n grid; time is constant in the number of seeds. The abstract notes the "errors due to the differences between the approximation and the actual Voronoi diagram are hardly noticeable to the naked eye"; JFA+1/JFA² variants reduce them further. This is the portable way to build Voronoi/distance fields in WGSL compute; the distance field can then be meshed.

**Output.** Convex-cell fragment meshes (angular). **Fidelity.** Excellent for man-made-looking angular breakage and gravel; combine with SDF weathering for natural stones. **Code.** Paper-only for Raghavachary; JFA has many GPU/WebGL/WGSL implementations to adapt. A real-time 2D Voronoi fracture thesis exists (diva-portal.org diva2:1452512).

#### 1c. Weathering / erosion as geometry
- **"Goblins by Spheroidal Weathering"** — Beardall, Farley, Ouderkirk, Reimschussel, Smith, Jones, Egbert (Eurographics Workshop on Natural Phenomena 2007, pp. 7–14). Voxel-based simulation using "bubbles centered on axis aligned voxels to approximate geometry-dependent effects of spheroidal weathering," producing concave rounded hoodoo/goblin forms that heightfields cannot represent, "at near interactive speeds." Explicitly parallelizable: the weathering rate at each voxel depends only on local geometry. Output: voxel field → mesh. Highly portable to a WGSL compute weathering pass over a voxel grid, then mesh. Follow-up: Jones et al., "Directable weathering of concave rock using curvature estimation" (IEEE TVCG 16(1):81–94, 2010).
- **GPU hydraulic erosion** — Mei, Decaudin, Hu, "Fast Hydraulic Erosion Simulation and Visualization on GPU" (Pacific Graphics 2007, DOI 10.1109/PG.2007.15). Virtual-pipe shallow-water model on a 2D grid storing terrain height, water height, suspended sediment, outflow flux, velocity; five update steps per iteration; "carefully designed to be implemented totally on GPU." Jákó & Tóth (CESCG 2011) add thermal erosion in the same virtual-pipe framework. Št'ava et al. (SCA 2008) interactive terrain erosion.
- **Sebastian Lague droplet erosion** (`github.com/SebLague/Hydraulic-Erosion`, MIT) — announcing the GPU compute port (X/Twitter, 2 Mar 2019), Lague states it is "now simulating 400k droplets of water running down a 1000x1000 map in about half a second." Particle/droplet model, easily ported to WGSL. These are primarily heightfield methods; apply to a rock's height/displacement or to a 3D field slice, then re-displace and mesh. The Kurzemnieks insight (below) — displacing the SDF directly rather than vertices along normals — avoids self-intersection and yields overhang-like geometry.

#### 1d. Structured / geology-based procedural rock modeling
- **Peytavie, Galin, Grosjean, Mérillou, "Procedural Generation of Rock Piles using Aperiodic Tiling"** (Computer Graphics Forum 28(7):1801–1809, 2009, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8659.2009.01557.x). Modified corner-cube aperiodic tiling (a complete set of "256 configurations"); Voronoï cells computed with a **parameterized anisotropic distance** to control contact shapes; "the final geometry of the rocks is created by eroding the surface of Voronoï cells almost everywhere but at some random contact points located on the faces." The abstract reports the method "has been successfully used to generate landscapes and stone huts and walls with thousands of rocks piled together" — "without any computationally demanding physically-based simulation." Output: meshes. Paper-only (a partial student reimplementation exists at grassovsky.wordpress.com). Portable but non-trivial.
- **Peytavie et al., "Arches: a Framework for Modeling Complex Terrains"** (CGF 28(2):457–467, 2009, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8659.2009.01385.x). Hybrid model: volumetric discrete material grid (rock/sand/air layers) + implicit surface (convolution of layers) for overhangs, arches, caves. Includes an original rock generation technique. Interactive editing. Output: implicit surface → mesh.
- **Paris et al., "Modeling Rocky Scenery using Implicit Blocks"** (The Visual Computer 2020, hal-02926218). Procedurally generates a fracture distribution in bedrock, defines tiling blocks as implicit volumetric primitives, replicates and blends them with generalized union/blending operators. Crucially: "the block generation algorithm can also produce a mesh representation Mk for every block Bk" — i.e. mesh output, streamable in production. Best geology-grounded method for cliffs/crags with correct block shapes.
- **Sakurai & Miyata** — Voronoi-cell rock piles/aggregates (CGF). **Dart, De Rossi, Togelius, "SpeedRock: procedural rocks through grammars and evolution"** (PCG Workshop / FDG 2011). Evolves 3D L-system rulesets; fitness = how well the collapsed structure matches a user shape; texture via raycasting from a bounding sphere; exports assets. Lightweight standalone tool; more of a curiosity than a fidelity leader.
- **Guérin et al. "Ghost Tile"** — density-field aggregate placement storing many overlapping candidate objects per tile with a precomputed collision graph, generating instances per user density fields (relevant for scattering rocks, not single-rock geometry).

#### 1e. Base-primitive displacement (assess honestly — often blobby)
Subdivided icosphere/cube displaced along normals by noise; plane/disk "scraping" for flat faces. **gl-rock** (Erikman/Erkaman, `github.com/Erkaman/gl-rock`, JS+GLSL) starts from a sphere, randomly projects vertices onto planes ("scraping") or disks (for concavity), then Perlin-distorts. **Honest assessment: produces blobby, low-quality rounded rocks** — the author's own texture is generated per-frame in a fragment shader (render-time, not baked). Value: it is a clean *wiring reference* for a JS mesh generator, NOT a quality target. Displacing along normals also causes self-intersection at high amplitude (the exact problem Kurzemnieks solves by displacing the SDF directly). Convex-hull-of-noisy-points is similarly limited to convex blobs. Use these only for the cheapest LODs or ignore them.

### CATEGORY 2 — TEXTURE-ONLY GENERATION (PBR maps as assets)

#### 2a. Substance 3D Designer rock graph vocabulary (STRONGEST texture recommendation)
**Technique.** A Substance rock material is a deterministic DAG of image operations, every one of which is a straightforward compute pass:
- **Cellular / Worley noise** and fractal-sum/Perlin bases for the height foundation.
- **Slope blur** (blur guided by a gradient/noise input) — the workhorse for eroded rock shapes.
- **Directional warp** and **non-directional warp** (Daniel Thiger's signature node) — displace UVs by a vector field to add organic breakup.
- **Flood Fill** (+ Flood Fill to Gradient/Random) to give each cell an ID for per-cell variation.
- **Height Blend** to combine layers by height.
- **Curvature / Ambient Occlusion from height**, **Normal from height** — all derived analytically from the height map.
- Edge damage / cracks / weathering passes layered on top.

**Best published breakdowns to replicate the graph structure:**
- **Daniel Thiger — Rock Creation Techniques 1 (Shapes) & 2 (Surface Detailing)** (dete.artstation.com/store) — the definitive rock-graph tutorials; Part 2 layers cracks/pits onto base shapes and is packaged as a reusable utility node.
- **80.lv breakdowns** — "Generating Concrete and Cracks in Substance Designer" (gradient-linear + Perlin warp → slope blur with Cloud2 → flood fill), and the Thiger-derived non-directional warp method.

**Output.** Baked albedo, normal, roughness, height/displacement, AO tiles. **Reimplementation.** Direct — each node = one WGSL compute kernel reading input texture(s), writing output texture. Deterministic, tile-friendly. **Performance.** Each pass is a full-screen kernel; a full rock material is tens of passes, milliseconds on GPU. **Fidelity.** Photorealistic; this is the industry standard. **Code.** No open rock graph, but node semantics are well documented; `github.com/tuxalin/procedural-tileable-shaders` provides tileable Worley/fbm/voronoi/perlin GLSL to port.

#### 2b. Pure analytic in-shader procedural materials (Inigo Quilez)
IQ's articles give production-quality GPU code for the building blocks: value/gradient/Voronoi noise, fBm, **domain warping**, **voronoise** (voronoi↔noise blend, `iquilezles.org/articles/voronoise/`), precise Voronoi borders, and **texture-repetition avoidance** (`iquilezles.org/articles/texturerepetition/` — scatter texture by a voronoi/hash pattern and blend, sampling ~9× to hide tiling). **Key constraint for this use case:** these are normally evaluated at render time; to fit "texture as asset" you must **render them once into an offscreen render target / storage texture and read back the baked maps**. That is trivial in WebGPU. **Fidelity.** Excellent, zero disk assets. **Code.** Copy-paste GLSL → WGSL.

#### 2c. Triplanar mapping for UV-free application
Essential because SDF-meshed rocks have no clean UVs. **Ben Golus, "Normal Mapping for a Triplanar Shader"** (bgolus.medium.com; `github.com/bgolus/Normal-Mapping-for-a-Triplanar-Shader`) is the definitive reference for *correct* normal blending across the three planar projections: **Whiteout blend** ("looks slightly better," ground-truth for axis-aligned faces), **UDN blend** (cheaper, slightly flattened), and **Reoriented Normal Mapping** ("fantastic… quite close to ground truth"). Naive swizzle and overlay/add are wrong. This applies both at render time and when baking a rock's maps into an atlas. Portable to WGSL directly.

#### 2d. Example-based / by-example texture synthesis
Patch-based and histogram-preserving stochastic synthesis (Heitz–Neyret style, referenced via the tuxalin repo and Jason Booth "Stochastic Texturing"), plus IQ texture-repetition, let a small rock photo tile infinitely without visible repetition. Reimplementable in JS/compute. Useful when starting from a scanned rock sample rather than pure noise.

#### 2e. Weathering/aging applied to textures
Image-space stone weathering, dirt/moisture/mineral staining passes (Dorsey et al. "Modeling and rendering of weathered stone," SIGGRAPH 1999, is the classic reference cited by the erosion literature). Implement as additional compute passes modulating albedo/roughness by cavity/AO/height masks derived from the height map.

#### 2f. Classic references
Ebert, Musgrave, Peachey, Perlin, Worley, *Texturing and Modeling: A Procedural Approach* (3rd ed.) — the foundational text for procedural rock/noise. GPU Gems / GPU Pro chapters on procedural terrain/rock texturing. Ryan Geiss "Generating Complex Procedural Terrains Using the GPU" (GPU Gems 3, ch.1) — 3D-noise density field + marching cubes on the GPU, directly analogous.

### CATEGORY 3 — BOTH TOGETHER (mesh + textures as integrated package)

#### 3a. SDF→mesh→derived-maps→triplanar-PBR blueprint (STRONGEST both-together recommendation)
The unifying insight: **when you generate a rock as an SDF, the same field and its gradient give you the maps for free.** Height/displacement = the field value near the surface; cavity/curvature = divergence/Laplacian or curvature of the field; AO = short-range occlusion sampled in the field; normal = field gradient. So a single pipeline emits mesh + height + curvature + cavity + AO, and albedo/roughness come from procedural passes (Category 2a/2b) masked by those maps, applied via triplanar (2c). This is the ideal architecture for a WebGPU rock generator and has no heavy dependencies.

#### 3b. Production Houdini generators (recipe proof; reimplement the algorithm)
- **Steven Skidmore / Gunfire Games, "Procedural Rock Generation in Houdini"** (80.lv breakdown `80.lv/articles/006sdf-breakdown-procedural-rock-generation-in-houdini`; ArtStation 8lYgGG). Recipe: blockout mesh → VDB/voxelmesh (SideFX Labs) → **Worley F2−F1 at multiple resolutions** (Y-frequency scaled up for vertically stretched cells) → **strata slicing** (planes copied to points, mesh sliced) with per-layer oriented-bounding-box transforms scaled on X/Z to keep **sharp strata edges** → **edge chipping** via low-res noisy mesh used to VDB-fracture the high-detail layers → low-poly shell by voxelizing at coarse density + polyReduce → **triplanar texturing** in Substance Painter. Skidmore reports it is "mostly Worley F2−F1 noises at different resolutions and a lot of VDB conversions in for-loops… about 5 minutes to generate the final high poly… All in all it takes about 9 minutes to go from blockout to final in game asset." Output: high+low poly + triplanar PBR. This is the single best-documented target recipe.
- **Gatis Kurzemnieks, "Creating procedural game assets with Houdini"** (rendereverything.com, 4 parts). Key technique: **work directly on the VDB SDF** — offset the "surface" attribute with 3D noise instead of displacing polygons along normals. In his words the "VDB SDF displace method produces even displacement in all cases… with heavier displacements it even starts to produce nice geometry overhangs — almost like vector displacement… no stretching because geometry is calculated at the end — after the displacement." This is the exact right lesson for an SDF-based WebGPU generator and explains why the field-then-mesh approach beats vertex displacement. Also covers VDB reshape/boolean for forming rock around colliders. (Part 3: COPs texture baking is "slow – very slow"; he moved to Substance Designer.)
- **kjohnsoninfo/HoudiniRockGenerator** (`github.com/kjohnsoninfo/HoudiniRockGenerator`, Python/HDA). Takes a base shape, outputs high-poly and low-poly, combined noise for blocky sharp cuts + horizontal striations, normal-map bake, auto-UV. Working code (Houdini), algorithm portable.
- **SideFX official Rock Generator** (sidefx.com/tutorials/rock-generator) and community SideFX gallery generator (deep/shallow cracks, horizontal/vertical stripes, VDB curvature generator from odforce, multi-threaded displacement ~1–2 min).

#### 3c. Web-native mesh+material generator
**gl-rock** (Erikman) — JS+GLSL, generates mesh (sphere scraping + Perlin) AND texture (Perlin in fragment shader). **Honest assessment: blobby, low-quality rounded rocks; texture is render-time, not baked.** It is the closest thing to a web-native end-to-end wiring reference but must not be used as a quality benchmark. Users have extended it (jMonkeyEngine forum) with per-face UV generation.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Build the SDF core (do this first).** Implement a WGSL field evaluator composing: IQ fBM-SDF sphere-grid base + Worley F2−F1 for cellular cracking + strata bands + domain warp, combined with smooth-min. This is the highest-leverage, highest-fidelity, most portable foundation and serves all three categories.

**Stage 2 — Add two meshers and switch per archetype.** Port Will Usher's WGSL Marching Cubes for smooth/weathered rocks (pebbles, boulders, spheroidally weathered forms) and a Dual Contouring / Manifold Dual Contouring mesher (adapt sdf2mesh's wgpu DC) for angular/fractured/strata rocks needing sharp edges. Benchmark: if edges look rounded when they should be crisp, you are on the wrong mesher — switch to (M)DC.

**Stage 3 — Derive maps from the field, texture via triplanar.** Emit height/curvature/cavity/AO from the field and gradient during meshing. Generate albedo/roughness with IQ analytic noise + Substance-style passes (slope blur, warp, flood fill, height blend) as WGSL compute, baked to storage textures. Apply with triplanar + Whiteout (or RNM for hero assets) normal blending. This delivers the "both together" package with no extra field cost.

**Stage 4 — Add fracture and weathering as optional passes.** For gravel/shards/angular boulders, add GPU Voronoi via Jump Flooding (log₂n passes) → half-space cell clipping or distance-field meshing. For natural rounding, add a Goblins-style voxel spheroidal-weathering compute pass and/or Mei/Lague-style erosion applied to the field (displace the SDF directly, per Kurzemnieks).

**Thresholds that change the plan:**
- If generation must be **fully real-time (<16 ms)** per rock: cap grid resolution (Marching Cubes memory is O(n³)), use surface nets chunking, and precompute Voronoi seed fields.
- If you need **millions of triangles** (Nanite): raise field resolution freely — that is the intended regime; watch GPU memory, mesh in chunks.
- If **sharp geology** (columnar basalt, stratified cliffs) dominates: prioritize Manifold Dual Contouring and the Skidmore strata-slice + edge-chip recipe.
- If **photoscanned realism** is required beyond procedural noise: add by-example synthesis (2d) from rock photos, and only then consider (out-of-scope) neural material capture.

## Caveats
- **Neural methods are explicitly out of scope**: single-image SVBRDF capture, diffusion PBR/3D generators, image-to-3D models require trained weights and heavy inference runtimes and cannot be reimplemented as pure WGSL/JS. Mentioned only as a labeled category; not recommended.
- **Render-time-only techniques don't satisfy the deliverable.** IQ's shaders and gl-rock's texture are evaluated per-frame by default; you MUST bake them into real meshes/texture maps (offscreen render / marching-cubes extraction) to meet the "asset" requirement.
- **Dual Contouring can produce non-manifold or holed meshes** at high resolution (sdf2mesh author confirms; the non-manifold issue is exactly what Manifold Dual Contouring was designed to fix); use MDC or post-process repair when watertightness matters.
- **Blobby-result approaches** (gl-rock, convex-hull-of-points, naive sphere displacement) are wiring references, not quality targets — flagged explicitly.
- **Houdini generation times (~5–9 min for Skidmore's high-poly) are not real-time**; a WebGPU reimplementation on modern GPUs should be far faster for equivalent resolution, but this is an engineering claim to validate, not a measured guarantee.
- Several key papers (Raghavachary, Peytavie aperiodic tiling, Arches, implicit blocks) are **paper-only** and require non-trivial reimplementation; only the meshing, erosion, JFA, and texture building blocks have ready GPU/WGSL/Rust code to adapt.
- Substance node semantics are documented in tutorials but there is **no open-source production rock graph**; you reimplement from the described structure.
